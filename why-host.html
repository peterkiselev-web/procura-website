Why host one

A flat phone
ends the visit

People keep their tickets, gym passes, bank cards and directions on their phones. When the battery goes, they leave, and they take their spending with them. These are the numbers behind that, from our own research and from UK surveys.

01 · Our research

We asked 10,000 gym members first

Before building the business we surveyed more than 10,000 gym members in 2025 and 2026. The answer was clear.

80%

said they would use a power bank at their gym, because they regularly run out of battery there.

02 · The UK picture

Most Britons are running close to empty

In June 2026 Vodafone UK asked 2,000 people about their phone batteries. On average they start to worry at 28%, and a third of Gen Z (36%) called a flat battery their most stressful everyday situation, ahead of traffic, running late and losing their keys.

Only 32% carry their own power bank. Everyone else is looking for a socket, and that usually means your socket or the door.

Source: Vodafone UK, survey of 2,000 UK adults by Walr, June 2026

03 · Screen time

4.5 hrs

UK adults now spend an average of four and a half hours online every day, and most of that time is on a smartphone.

That's a lot of battery gone before anyone reaches your front door.

Source: Ofcom, Online Nation 2025, published 10 December 2025

04 · What people do about it

They change their plans

9 in 10

smartphone users said they felt panic when their battery dropped to 20% or lower.

32%

said they would drop everything and turn round to go home and charge.

42%

of millennials said they would likely skip the gym if it came down to working out or charging their phone.

Source: LG Electronics Low Battery Anxiety survey, 2,000 US smartphone users, conducted by Ebiquity, 2016

05 · What it means for your venue

Where a station earns its space

Gyms

The code to get in and out is on the phone. When it dies, the member is stuck at the barrier asking staff to let them out. It happens on the way out, so it's the last thing they remember about the visit.

Cafés and co-working

The two or three sockets are taken by 10am, and people pick the plug over the table. With a bank to hand, the whole room becomes usable, and the customer who would have left at 3pm stays until they've finished, ordering as they go.

Hotels, bars and transport hubs

Guests need their phones for tickets, taxis, room keys and the way home. A bank they can take with them solves the problem without anyone guarding a cable behind the desk.
